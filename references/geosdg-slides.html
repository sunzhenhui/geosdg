Background

The Spatial Challenge of the SDGs

  • 17 Goals · 169 Targets · 230+ indicators — a shared framework for global resource & environmental challenges.
  • Many goals are inherently spatial, directly tied to land use and infrastructure conditions.
  • Turning global agendas into regional action demands spatially explicit, forward-looking evidence.
  • The 2030 deadline is near and progress lags; scholars propose extending the framework to 2050 (mid-term 2030 + terminal 2050) — requiring scenario projection, not just retrospective assessment.

Background

Limitations of Existing Approaches

① Retrospective

Existing SDG scores rely on historical statistics, lacking dynamic simulation of future trends and policy interventions.

② Non-spatial

Statistical / data-driven frameworks (iSDG, IFs, SDG-IO, T21) ignore spatial variability and process-driven spatial dynamics.

③ Single-domain

Existing CA models simulate land use only, omitting infrastructure dynamics; no framework couples both with spatial SDG assessment.

→ This research gap motivates GeoSDG.

Method

Land-Use Simulation: CALUCC

Ptotal = PRF × Ωneighborhood × Inertia × Constraints × Stochastic  (Eq. 3–5)
  • Cellular automata with random forest (RF) learning transition rules from spatial drivers.
  • Adaptive inertia coefficient balances supply & demand; roulette-wheel selection.
  • This is where "AI" enters the model: RF learns driver→land-use-type mapping.

Infrastructure Simulation: CAINFRA

  • Two-state CA (covered / uncovered) for infrastructure.
  • RF estimates coverage probability + competitive patch-seeding (Eq. 6–7).
  • Highlight: first to simulate land use and infrastructure coverage simultaneously.

Case · Boundaries

The Toolkit Is Not a Panacea

Complement, not substitute

Weak correlation for SDG 2/7/9/13 reflects the limit of spatial proxies for complex socioeconomic processes → GeoSDG should complement, not replace, statistical monitoring. (Discussion D3, part 2)

Transferability is conceptual

Modular structure can adapt to data-limited regions, but so far validated only in the YRDUA. Application needs local data + recalibration + adapted indicators/thresholds/weights. (Discussion D4)

Tool Evolution · v1.0

The Original GUI Release

A2 · Paper Figure 2GeoSDG GUI screenshot
  • Windows desktop GUI, implemented in C++, open-source (GPL-3.0).
  • Released with the paper: figshare DOI: 10.6084/m9.figshare.29445089.v3.

Limitations

Windows-bound · desktop interaction paradigm · hard to integrate with AI / automation workflows · higher bar for reproduction & re-engineering.

Tool Evolution · v2.0

The Scriptable CLI

  • Core algorithms (indicator computation, CA simulation, priority-area identification) refactored into a standalone, cross-platform CLI.
  • Built on the GDAL raster ecosystem.
  • Scriptable & reproducible — integrates into batch pipelines and third-party systems.

↳ Programmability is the prerequisite for AI automation.

Conclusion

Takeaways & Outlook

1

Spatially explicit, forward-looking tools are key to making SDGs actionable.

2

GeoSDG couples land-use & infrastructure CA with SSP scenarios for assessment.

3

From GUI → CLI → AI Agent, GeoSDG is evolving toward an open, reproducible, intelligent form.

Limitations: 27 indicators · validated only in YRDUA · long-term projection uncertainty. Outlook: cross-region validation, indicator expansion, a smarter Agent version (in development).
